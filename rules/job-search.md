# Job Search — Professional Content Rules

## Resume / CV

- Tailor each resume to the specific job description
- Lead with impact, not responsibilities: "Increased X by Y%" not "Responsible for X"
- Quantify achievements wherever possible (%, $, time saved, users served)
- Keep to 1 page for <10 years experience, 2 pages for senior+
- Use reverse-chronological order
- ATS-friendly: use standard section headings, no tables/columns/graphics

## Cover letter

- Open with the specific role and why this company
- 3 paragraphs max: hook, value proposition, call to action
- Mirror the job posting's language and keywords
- Reference a specific company project, product, or value
- Never apologize for what you lack — emphasize what you bring

## LinkedIn profile

- Headline = role + value proposition, not just job title
- About section: 3–5 lines, first-person, ends with a call to action
- Experience bullets: same impact-first format as resume
- Skills section: top 3 relevant skills matching target roles
- Customize URL: linkedin.com/in/your-name

## Networking

- Personalize every outreach message — no copy-paste templates
- Reference a specific connection point (shared interest, mutual contact, their work)
- Ask for advice, not jobs: "What would you recommend for someone with my background?"
- Follow up once after 5–7 business days — don't chase
- Give before you ask: share an article, congratulate an achievement, offer help

## Interview prep

- STAR format for behavioral questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result
- Prepare 3–5 stories that demonstrate different competencies
- Research the company's recent news, products, and challenges
- Prepare thoughtful questions that show genuine curiosity
- Practice salary negotiation with specific numbers, not ranges

## Job boards and applications

- Apply within 24–48 hours of posting — early applicants get priority
- Track applications in a spreadsheet: company, role, date, status, follow-up
- Quality over quantity — 5 tailored applications beat 50 generic ones
- Check company careers pages directly — not all roles are posted on boards
- Use "Easy Apply" sparingly — direct applications with tailored resumes perform better

## Portfolio / work samples

- Show 3–5 best projects, not everything you've ever made
- Each project: problem, approach, result, link to live/demo
- Case studies > screenshots: explain the thinking behind the design/code
- Include a variety: solo work, team projects, different technologies
- Keep it updated — remove anything older than 2 years unless it's landmark work
